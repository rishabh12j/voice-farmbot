# GrowMate — Demo Day Presentation (Draft 1)

**Audience:** 10 elders, 3 groups × 30 min, Dundalk focus group
**Date:** 9 June 2026
**Format:** 10 slides, **18-point sans-serif minimum**, printed handouts on the table
**Run time:** 4-5 minutes spoken intro before the hands-on segment

---

## Slide 1 — Title

```
🌱 GrowMate
A garden robot you talk to.

Rishabh Jain · Maynooth University
Supervisor: Dr Majid Sorouri
```

**Speaker notes (30 s):** Welcome them by name where possible. Say what you'll do today: "We're going to show you a robot that listens to your voice. Then you'll get to try talking to it. At the end I'll ask a few short questions about what worked and what didn't."

---

## Slide 2 — Why we're building this

```
For people who'd love a garden but can't always
get out to tend one.

· Movement is hard
· Bending hurts
· Memory plays tricks

What if the garden helped instead?
```

**Speaker notes (45 s):** Frame the audience in. Don't dwell on disability — focus on dignity. "If you can describe what you want, the robot does the bending."

---

## Slide 3 — What FarmBot is

```
[ photo of FarmBot Genesis XL on the bed ]

A small motorised gantry that runs along a
raised bed and waters, photographs, and
tends plants.

GrowMate adds a voice on top of it.
```

**Speaker notes (45 s):** Point at the actual robot if it's visible. Explain it never leaves its bed — that's the safety boundary. Mention it's not new — FarmBot has been around for years; what's new is the voice layer.

---

## Slide 4 — Safety first

```
🛑  EMERGENCY STOP

If anything looks wrong, press the
red button. Everything halts immediately.

The robot can't move outside its bed.
```

**Speaker notes (30 s):** Show the e-stop on screen and demonstrate pressing it. **Critical:** mention that "stop" works as a spoken word too — "you don't have to find the button". Re-state that the bed has hard limits the robot literally cannot cross.

---

## Slide 5 — How you'll talk to it

```
Three steps:

1. Press the green microphone button
2. Say what you want
3. Watch the robot show you what it understood
   before it does it
```

**Speaker notes (45 s):** Emphasise the third step — this is the research contribution but for them it just means "the robot tells you what it heard before it moves". This is what the elderly users with memory concerns will appreciate.

---

## Slide 6 — What you can ask for today

```
Try things like:

· "Take a photo"
· "Go home"
· "Water the tomatoes"
· "Move to the herbs"
· "Stop"

Don't worry about exact words — try your own.
```

**Speaker notes (30 s):** Encourage variation. "If your way of saying it doesn't work, that's information for me — please tell me what you tried."

---

## Slide 7 — What you'll do today (the deal)

```
✓ Try at least 2 commands
✓ Tell us when something feels confusing
✓ Fill in a short questionnaire (we'll help)

You can stop or step away at any time.
```

**Speaker notes (30 s):** Make consent feel ordinary, not heavy. Mention there are no right answers and the questionnaire is short.

---

## Slide 8 — What we'll measure

```
We're learning whether this is:

· Safe to use without supervision
· Easy to understand without training
· Worth using in your own garden

Honest answers — even harsh ones — are the
most useful kind.
```

**Speaker notes (30 s):** Inviting honesty is research-critical. Elderly participants tend to be polite; explicitly grant permission to be critical.

---

## Notes for the presenter

- **Print 4 copies of the deck per group** in landscape, single page each, large fonts. The screen is the demo, not the slides.
- **Don't read the slides verbatim** — use them as scaffolding.
- **Pause for confirmation** after slide 4 (safety) and slide 9 (consent). Make eye contact.
- **If a participant looks lost on slide 5**, switch tabs to the manual control panel to show them the d-pad alternative — gives them an out.
- **Total slot budget:** intro slides ≈ 5 min, researcher demo ≈ 3 min, hands-on ≈ 12 min, questionnaire ≈ 8 min, Q&A ≈ 2 min.
